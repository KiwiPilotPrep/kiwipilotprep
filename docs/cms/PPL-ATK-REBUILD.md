# PPL Aircraft Technical Knowledge — the rebuild

Generated by `scripts/ppl-report.mjs` from the extracted source, the curriculum in
`content/ppl/aircraft-technical-knowledge.mjs`, and the rows in the database. If the three
disagree, the script fails rather than writing this file.

## What changed

This subject had no course. Forty-two published CAA syllabus topics, two hundred and ten items,
fourteen fragments of study content, **no chapters and no lessons** — its material had been
attached to the syllabus rows themselves, so a student who had paid for PPL theory opened
Aircraft Technical Knowledge and was handed the regulator's examination checklist.

| Chapters | 0 | 38 |

| Content blocks | 0 | 2420 |
| Diagrams | 0 | 342 |
| Lesson → syllabus item mappings | 0 | 2134 |

The syllabus rows are archived, not deleted: every row, every id and every mapping survives,
and `scripts/ppl-visibility.mjs --revert` puts the status back. The subject id is unchanged, so
entitlements keyed on it and every existing route keep working.

Of the 2420 blocks a student now reads, 2013 are the source book's own words
and 407 are authored teaching apparatus — the lead paragraph that says what a topic is
for, key points, worked examples, what to hold on to, and where people go wrong. Every block
carries which of the two it is, so they never blur together in later editing.

## The diagrams

The source holds 579 distinct images. **341 of them reach a student** — 342 placements across the course — while 224 are rejected, and 12 are turned the right way up before they are stored.

Every one was put on a contact sheet and looked at, which mattered: the measurement pass had
flagged 205 images as mirrored heading reflections and thirteen of them turned out to be real
diagrams — the angle of attack sequence on page 34, the flaps-up-flaps-down camber comparison on
page 43, the control column drawing on page 308. Those are kept. What is rejected is
overwhelmingly one artefact: 186 pale upside-down WordArt copies of chapter headings, which
carry nothing at all. The rest are named one at a time in
`content/ppl/diagram-decisions.mjs` — a stock library watermark, another academy's wordmark, a
presenter's photograph, a bare red arrow that pointed at something on a page it is no longer on.

The twelve rotated figures are a different fault. A PDF may embed an image any way round and
place it with a transform; the extractor gets the stored bytes. Those twelve read perfectly in
the book and arrived here upside down — a bourdon tube with "pointer shaft" written upside down
under it. They are real teaching diagrams, so the build rotates them rather than dropping them.

## The consistency polish, after the other five

The five remaining PPL subjects were rebuilt afterwards, and the polish they
received went further than ATK's first pass had. This subject was brought up
to the same standard in one focused pass. Nothing about the curriculum moved:

- Source claims are **unchanged** — 444 pages claimed, 62 skipped, 0 duplicate
  and 0 unclaimed, the same 38 chapters and 270 lessons, the same 2,013 source
  blocks, the same slugs, ids and 2,134 mappings.
- Diagrams are **unchanged** — 341 distinct figures of 579, 216 rejected. No
  image was added, removed or re-decided.

What changed is the authored layer, from 407 blocks to 432, and one source
artefact:

- **25 lessons** flagged by the student-perspective audit were given what they
  were short of: a figure caption where the picture was carrying the teaching
  and nothing explained it, a short context or takeaway where a topic was a
  bare list, and framing on the two long topics that ran to a page of source
  text with only an introduction. The audit that flagged them now returns zero
  across all six subjects.
- **One source artefact removed.** Page 287 ended a sentence with "(See next
  slide)" — a pointer into a book the student is not holding. It is in
  `SUBSTITUTIONS` in `source-repairs.mjs` like every other page-keyed repair,
  and the sentence it hung off is untouched.

The additions follow the same rule as the rest of the subject: the source
first, bounded explanation only where a student would otherwise be left with a
term, a number or a diagram that nothing accounts for, and provenance kept —
every one carries `origin: "authored"` and none carries a source citation.
