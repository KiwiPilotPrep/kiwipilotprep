# Flight Test Groundwork — the build

Generated by `scripts/groundwork-report.mjs` from the supplied documents, the curricula in
`content/groundwork/` and the rows in the database. If the three disagree, the script fails
rather than writing this file.

## The eight modules

The PRD (§3.2) prescribes eight modules for Flight Test Groundwork and requires PPL and CPL
to use the same eight. They are modules, not theory subjects, and the list lives in
`PRESCRIBED_MODULES` in `scripts/build-groundwork-course.mjs`; both curricula are checked
against it and a build that renames or reorders one is rejected.

Every one of those blocks is accounted for exactly once. The build refuses to finish
otherwise, and `scripts/groundwork-validate.mjs` adds the same total up again from outside
so a block cannot go missing between the two. A block is either taught, on a page skipped
with a written reason, or removed as the document's own furniture — a page number, a running
head, or a caption for a photograph that is not shown.

### Diagrams

47 images came out of the four documents. 21 are shown, 23 were rejected
and 3 sit on pages that are skipped in their entirety. Each rejection has its reason
recorded against the image's SHA-1 in
`content/groundwork/ppl-diagrams.mjs`, so the decision survives re-extraction. The rejected
ones are the word processor's own bullet icons and template placeholder, the airspace
booklet's stock photography, and one table supplied twice.

### The Examiner Hot Seat

The supplied questions document contains oral questions written as prompt, common trap and
model answer. They are study material, not a question bank: there are no options and no key,
and the app's `Question` rows are single-choice, so turning them into questions would mean
inventing wrong answers. They are therefore the last topic of each module, in the form they
were written, and nothing was added to them.

## CPL

No CPL Flight Test Groundwork material has been supplied. The eight modules exist, each with
a summary describing what it will cover and a note saying the material is in preparation, and
there are no topics. Nothing was copied from PPL — a commercial candidate reading PPL
privileges and PPL fuel reserves would be studying the wrong requirements — and nothing was
written to fill the gap, because teaching that looks like client material but is not is worse
than an empty module that says so.

When the material arrives, `content/groundwork/cpl-flight-test.mjs` says what to do with it.

## One table the source cannot establish

The airspace classification table in the study document is flattened past the point where
its cells can be put back. Class D prints three flight rules and seven statements where six
are needed; Class C prints six that do not divide the way its column order implies. Any grid
built from them would be a guess about which service belongs to which flight rule, which is
the one thing not worth guessing on a page about separation.

So the class labels are separated from the words the PDF welded to them, the statements are
left exactly as the client wrote them, and no grid is invented. The whole of the
classification material — Class G, Class D and Class C — is read in one topic rather than
split across two, which is where the document happened to print it.

This is a source-quality issue rather than a build one. Closing it properly needs the
original table from the client.

## Checked against the flight test itself

The authority is the CAA's own form — **Part 61 private pilot licence, aeroplane flight
test report, CAA 24061-22** — and `scripts/groundwork-caa-check.mjs` reconciles the eight
modules against it. The first eight tasks the examiner marks on that form are the eight
modules the PRD prescribes, with the same names in the same order, and every objective the
form prints beside them is checked against what the module actually teaches.

The twenty-three tasks after those eight are flown rather than briefed and are outside this
product. They are listed by the check rather than folded into a module where they would not
belong. The six written examinations the form records dates for are PPL Theory, a separate
course.

The check is internal. Nothing it produces reaches a student, and the form's wording never
appears on a page.

Three of the objectives the form names were not reached by the supplied material: securing
the load, the interior check, and load security. They are closed with authored context on
the topics a student is already reading when the question arises, in the same bounded way
the theory courses add explanation. The regulatory statements in that context come from the
current Part 91 consolidation — 91.213 carry-on baggage, 91.215 carriage of cargo, 91.219
familiarity with limitations and emergency equipment — and everything else defers to the
flight manual for the type rather than asserting a procedure of its own. No aircraft-specific
procedure, limitation or number is invented, and none of it is attributed to the client.

## What was left alone

The courses, the subjects, the products that grant them and the entitlements that reference
them all existed already; this build looks them up and never creates them. The chapter rows
were kept with their ids and their progress, and only their content was rewritten — they had
been seeded with placeholder text telling students the real material is uploaded through the
admin console.

16 archived subjects remain on the two courses, left from an earlier attempt that
modelled the eight modules as subjects. They are empty, archived and unreachable, and are
kept rather than deleted so nothing that once referenced them breaks.
